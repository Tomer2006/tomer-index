How the index is built

Methodology

What the Tomer index measures

The Tomer index is an HDI-style composite of health, income, and safety. Each pillar is normalised to 0–1, then combined into a single weighted geometric mean. The default display rescales the result to 0–100 (toggleable to 0–10 or 0–1 in the header).

Pillars and goalposts

Health (½ life expectancy + ½ HALE)

Both inputs use UNDP's life-table goalposts: 20 years (worst) and 85 years (best). The pillar averages life expectancy (LE) and healthy life expectancy (HALE), so a country is rewarded for both living long and living well. 

LEI(years) = (clamp(years, 20, 85) − 20) / (85 − 20)
Health = ½·LEI(LE) + ½·LEI(HALE)

Income (GNI per capita, log-scaled)

World Bank GNI per capita in 2021-PPP dollars is log-transformed and mapped to 0–1 between $100 and $75,000 — the same goalposts UNDP uses for HDI. Log scaling means each doubling of income adds a fixed amount, not a fixed fraction. 

Income = (ln(clamp(GNI, 100, 75000)) − ln(100)) / (ln(75000) − ln(100))

Safety (homicide-only inversion)

Intentional homicides per 100,000 inverted linearly with a 60/100k ceiling. Only homicides are used because property crime, assault, and sexual-violence statistics are not comparable across countries (definitions, reporting culture, and survey methodology differ too much). 

Safety = (60 − clamp(homicides, 0, 60)) / 60

Weights

The three pillars are combined as a weighted geometric mean with ratio 4 : 4 : 1 (health : income : safety). Safety is intentionally less influential because the homicide proxy is narrower than the other two pillars. 

Tomer = Health4/9 · Income4/9 · Safety1/9

Geometric (rather than arithmetic) means penalise imbalance: a country can't compensate for a near-zero pillar by excelling elsewhere.

Data sources

  • Life expectancy — World Bank SP.DYN.LE00.IN.
  • HALE (healthy life expectancy at birth, both sexes) — WHO Global Health Observatory WHOSIS_000002. The only non-World-Bank input.
  • GNI per capita, PPP (constant 2021 international $) — World Bank NY.GNP.PCAP.PP.KD.
  • Intentional homicides per 100,000 — World Bank VC.IHR.PSRC.P5 (originally UNODC).
  • Population (timeline weights) — World Bank SP.POP.TOTL.
  • Country / region / income / lending metadata — World Bank country API.

Year coverage and back-fill

The leaderboard supports any year from 2000 to 2023. For each displayed year we use the latest source observation on or before that year; if no prior observation exists, we fall back to the earliest later one. The actual source year for each metric is stored on every data point and surfaced in entry-page tooltips, so you can see when a value was carried forward from another year.

Notable gaps: World Bank's NY.GNP.PCAP.PP.KD series for some countries (most visibly China) only publishes one or two years, so those entries show a flat GNI line over their timeline.

Derived rows

"World", regional, income-group, and lending-type rows are population-weighted aggregates computed on the fly from country observations. They are not separate published series.

Limitations

  • Safety is homicide-only; countries with low homicide but high other-crime rates may be over-rated for "safety".
  • GNI is reported in constant 2021 PPP, so cross-year movement within a country reflects real growth — but countries with sparse source data effectively have a constant GNI in the chart.
  • The Tomer index is not endorsed by UNDP, the World Bank, or WHO. It's a personal composite that mirrors HDI's structure with safety added.
