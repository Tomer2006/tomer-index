How the index is built

Methodology

What the Tomer index measures

The Tomer index combines the four things that make a country great: abundance, safety, health, and freedom. Each pillar is normalised to 0–1, then combined into a single weighted geometric mean. The default display rescales the result to 0–100 (toggleable to 0–10 or 0–1 in the header).

Pillars and goalposts

Health (½ life expectancy + ½ HALE)

Both inputs use UNDP's life-table goalposts: 20 years (worst) and 85 years (best). The pillar averages life expectancy (LE) and healthy life expectancy (HALE), so a country is rewarded for both living long and living well. 

LEI(years) = (clamp(years, 20, 85) − 20) / (85 − 20)
Health = ½·LEI(LE) + ½·LEI(HALE)

When WHO does not report HALE for a displayed year, the tracker adjusts it using life expectancy rather than keeping HALE flat. It preserves the latest reported gap between life expectancy and HALE: 

Estimated HALE = current LE − (LE at latest HALE year − latest reported HALE)

Estimated HALE is capped at current life expectancy and labeled HALE est. with the latest reported HALE source year.

Abundance (GNI preferred, GDP fallback; log-scaled)

World Bank GNI per capita in 2021-PPP dollars is preferred for each country-year. When that annual GNI observation is unavailable, GDP per capita in the same constant 2021-PPP units is used as a fallback. The selected value is mapped to 0–1 between $100 and $75,000 — the same goalposts UNDP uses for HDI. Log scaling means each doubling of income adds a fixed amount, not a fixed fraction. 

Abundance value = GNI PPP if available, otherwise GDP PPP
Abundance = (ln(clamp(value, 100, 75000)) − ln(100)) / (ln(75000) − ln(100))

Safety (homicide-only inversion)

Intentional homicides per 100,000 inverted linearly with a 60/100k ceiling. Only homicides are used because property crime, assault, and sexual-violence statistics are not comparable across countries (definitions, reporting culture, and survey methodology differ too much). 

Safety = (60 − clamp(homicides, 0, 60)) / 60

Freedom (Human Freedom Index personal freedom)

Freedom is derived from the Personal Freedom sub-index of the Cato Institute and Fraser Institute's Human Freedom Index 2025. It averages rule of law, movement, religion, association, expression and information, and relationships. The Security and Safety category is deliberately excluded so it does not overlap the Safety pillar. 

Freedom score = 10 × mean(Rule of law, Movement, Religion,
                          Association, Expression, Relationships)
Freedom pillar = Freedom score / 100

Weights

Before weighting, each pillar is normalized to 0–1 against the fixed goalposts above. The normalized values go directly into the geometric mean; there is no yearly percentile-ranking step. Scores are therefore absolute and comparable across countries and years: a country's score does not change merely because another country's performance changes.

The four distinct dimensions are weighted by importance: Health 40%, Safety 30%, Freedom 20%, and Abundance 10%. 

Tomer = Health0.40 · Safety0.30 · Freedom0.20 · Abundance0.10

Geometric (rather than arithmetic) means penalise imbalance: a country can't compensate for a near-zero pillar by excelling elsewhere. Because every pillar uses fixed goalposts before combination, raw units do not control the result. Approximately, a 1% relative improvement in one pillar changes the final index by that pillar's weight times 1%, holding the other pillars fixed.

Data sources

  • Life expectancy — World Bank SP.DYN.LE00.IN.
  • HALE (healthy life expectancy at birth, both sexes) — WHO Global Health Observatory WHOSIS_000002. The only non-World-Bank input.
  • GNI per capita, PPP (constant 2021 international $) — World Bank NY.GNP.PCAP.PP.KD, the preferred income measure.
  • GDP per capita, PPP (constant 2021 international $) — World Bank NY.GDP.PCAP.PP.KD, used only for country-years without a GNI observation.
  • Intentional homicides per 100,000 — World Bank VC.IHR.PSRC.P5 (originally UNODC).
  • Population (timeline weights) — World Bank SP.POP.TOTL.
  • Personal Freedom categories, excluding Security and Safety — Human Freedom Index 2025, copublished by the Cato Institute and Fraser Institute; rescaled from 0–10 to 0–100.
  • Country / region / income / lending metadata — World Bank country API.

Year coverage and back-fill

The leaderboard supports any year from 2000 to 2024. For each displayed year we use the latest source observation on or before that year, looking back as far as 2000; later observations are never pulled backward, and the data pipeline does not ingest observations newer than 2024 at all (recent vintages may still be revised). The actual source year for each metric is stored on every data point and shown next to every value on the leaderboard — muted when it matches the displayed year, highlighted when the value was carried forward from an earlier year.

HALE is the exception to flat carry-forward: missing HALE years are adjusted using the life-expectancy change described above. Future WHO HALE observations are never pulled backward.

GNI is selected before GDP independently for each country-year. The source badge identifies whether a displayed income value is GNI, GDP, or a mixed population-weighted aggregate.

Derived rows

"World", regional, income-group, and lending-type rows are population-weighted aggregates computed on the fly from country observations. They are not separate published series.

Comparable trend chart

The headline trend uses a fixed cohort: only countries with a complete index for every year from 2000 through 2024 are included, and the same countries remain in the calculation throughout. Population weights still change normally over time. This prevents a country entering the dataset from creating a false rise or fall. The chart states its cohort size and coverage and is not labeled as a whole-world estimate.

Limitations

  • Safety is homicide-only; countries with low homicide but high other-crime rates may be over-rated for "safety".
  • Freedom is a custom recombination of published HFI category scores, not the HFI's published Personal Freedom headline score.
  • Both income series use constant 2021 PPP, so cross-year movement within a country reflects real growth. GNI and GDP are related but not identical measures, so fallback transitions can create a break in an otherwise continuous income series.
  • The Tomer index is not endorsed by UNDP, the World Bank, or WHO. It's a personal composite built around abundance, safety, health, and freedom.
